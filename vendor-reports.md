# Vendor reports and status

Last updated: **2026-10-16**. "Filed" means submitted or published. It does not mean a vendor has acknowledged, reproduced or fixed anything.

## Intel

| Channel | Visibility | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [Intel Community, Wireless board](https://community.intel.com/t5/Wireless/Intel-ICPS-driver-zeroes-IPv6-Flow-Label-mid-connection-breaking/m-p/1760647) | Public | Posted 2026-10-02. |
| Intel Customer Support case | Private | Opened 2026-10-02. On 2026-10-06 Intel asked for a test with its generic ICPS package. Results sent 2026-10-07: the generic 50.26.623.243 does not have the bug. Asked Intel which version fixed it, to get the fix to OEMs and Windows Update, and to publish an advisory. On 2026-10-07, sent Intel the list of other affected laptop makers and organizations. Intel Customer Support replied (by 2026-10-08) that it had passed requests to its team for an advisory naming the affected and first fixed versions, notice to every OEM shipping affected builds to supersede them on Windows Update and in their own tools, work with Microsoft to stop Windows Update offering the broken builds, and an engineering escalation to confirm which driver version fixed the flow label and why the release notes don't say so. Intel promised a status update by 2026-10-13. On 2026-10-13 Intel replied that the installed package is Samsung's OEM build, recommended asking Samsung for its latest OEM version, and said that the advisory, OEM notification and Microsoft coordination requests fall within Samsung's scope as the OEM. In the same reply Intel acknowledged in writing, citing the owner's tests, that its generic 50.26.623.243 (driver 12.10.14.33) does not zero the flow label. The same day, Intel was asked, as the maker of the driver, whether driver 11.5.11.19 in the OEM package is Intel's own unmodified build, which driver version first keeps the flow label, whether Intel has told the laptop makers, and what came of each request it listed on 2026-10-08. A written answer was requested by 2026-10-21. On 2026-10-14 Intel replied that "The information previously shared represents the full extent of what we are currently able to provide", and, on the request for a contact at a higher level, that the owner "will be reached out to as soon as possible". No such contact as of 2026-10-16. |

| Microsoft Executive Customer Relations | Private | Letter to the Office of the CEO sent 2026-10-07, asking for a named owner for the support case. On 2026-10-08, a relationship manager from Executive Customer Relations opened a new case. After a phone call on 2026-10-09, the full write-up was sent the same day. The owner asked Microsoft to consider a goodwill gesture in recognition of the root cause that Microsoft's own engineers are acting on, and cited no contract, license or warranty. On 2026-10-14, Executive Customer Relations turned the request down, called its decision final, and named no review channel, reference, owner or date for a Windows Update fix. The owner asked for a review of the decision by an Executive Customer Relations manager, for the reference, owning team and Windows Update date of the engineering review, and for how to deliver the logs. On 2026-10-16 it replied by citing the warranty terms of the Windows license, under which remedies are limited to repair, replacement or refund, and any recovery that the law still allows is limited to the amount paid for Windows, or up to US$50 if Windows was obtained without charge. It wrote that "Microsoft did not commission or enter into an agreement to purchase your investigation, testing, or findings", and that its decision "will not be reconsidered through this case". The reply was signed by the same relationship manager and named no manager to review the decision, and no engineering reference, owning team or date. For the logs, it sent a ten-step guide to Feedback Hub, where the owner had filed two reports with the full root cause on 2026-10-02, as the owner had told it on 2026-10-14. It added: "A submission does not guarantee an individual response, an engineering owner, or a remediation date." |
| Azure Networking engineering | Private | Packet-level evidence sent to Azure Front Door engineering leadership on 2026-10-07. On 2026-10-08, Microsoft confirmed the problem: when a client changes the flow label after the SYN, routers that hash on the label send the rest of the connection to a different Azure Front Door server. Microsoft said it has asked its team to work with the Windows team on a fix, and is working on the Azure Front Door side to reduce the impact. No date given. A request for a rough timeline, sent the same day, has had no answer as of 2026-10-16. |
| Microsoft support escalation | Private | On 2026-10-08, a senior Microsoft leader passed the support case to his escalation team, which "should be reaching out shortly".
